## Reading these numbers honestly

- The corpus is small and was written for this demo, so a high hit rate shows the pipeline works end to end, not that it generalises.
- Zero invalid citations is structural in offline mode: the extractive writer only emits passages it retrieved. The validator matters when an LLM writes the answer.
- With only 4 chunks, any reasonable retriever finds the expected source. The public benchmark below is the one that measures retrieval quality.

## BEIR SciFact retrieval benchmark

Generated by `python eval/beir_scifact.py`. 5,183 scientific abstracts, 300 test queries with expert relevance labels. Deterministic; timings from a 2-core cloud CPU.

| Pipeline | NDCG@10 | vs BM25 | Recall@10 | Recall@100 | ms / query (CPU) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BM25 (keyword baseline) | 0.652 | +0.0% | 0.776 | 0.873 | 15 |
| Dense: bge-small + Qdrant | 0.706 | +8.2% | 0.830 | 0.943 | 19 |
| Dense + cross-encoder re-rank (top 30) | 0.701 | +7.5% | 0.832 | 0.943 | 2743 |

Re-ranking covers the top 30 vector-search candidates per query, the same depth the app uses. Embedding model `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, re-ranker `Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`.

### Caveats

- SciFact is one domain (biomedical claims). Other BEIR datasets would give different numbers.
- The BM25 baseline uses rank-bm25 with plain lowercase tokenisation; the BEIR paper reports 0.665 for Elasticsearch BM25. Our bge-small score (0.706) is close to the 0.713 reported for it on the MTEB leaderboard, which suggests the setup is sound.
- The cross-encoder did not help on SciFact and is off by default in the app.
- The paraphrase set is small and hand-written, and the similarity floor was chosen on it.
